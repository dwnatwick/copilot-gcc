## Copilot for Word and PowerPoint deep dive

**Starter prompt in Copilot chat**

Using only files in our SharePoint site, list the source documents you can reference about the procurement modernization initiative. For each, give the title, date, and a one-line summary. Flag any document that appears outdated or superseded.

### Word Prompts 

**Executive Summary**

Review this document and create a one-page executive summary for senior leadership. Include key accomplishments, risks, budget impacts, and recommended next actions. Limit the summary to 500 words and use formal government language.

**Stakeholder re-write**

Rewrite this document for executive leadership. Focus on outcomes, risks, and business impact. Remove technical detail and use concise language. Then produce a legislative-briefing version.

**Meeting notes to project charter**

Using the meeting notes in this document, create a formal project charter with objectives, scope, deliverables, timeline, risks, and stakeholders. Mark anything the notes do not cover as 'to be confirmed' rather than inventing detail. 

**Gap analysis and recommendations**

Review this policy document. Identify gaps, ambiguities, duplicate content, and areas needing clarification. Present findings in a table with the section reference, the issue, and a recommended revision.

**Compare document changes**

Compare these two versions of the policy and summarize major changes, new requirements, removed content, and potential stakeholder impacts. Note anything affecting compliance. 

### PowerPoint Prompts

**Create PowerPoint from a Word document**

Create a concise executive presentation from this document. Include overview, key findings, risks, recommendations, and next steps. Target audience: executive leadership. Keep it to ten slides with no more than five bullets per slide.

**Change target audience**

Modify this presentation for department executives. Reduce technical content and emphasize business outcomes, risks, costs, and strategic impact. Keep the existing template and slide design.

**Generate Presenter notes**

Generate presenter notes for each slide. Each note should be a one to two minute speaking narrative suitable for a leadership audience, ending with a transition line into the next slide. 

**Improve an existing slide deck**

Review this presentation and recommend improvements to story flow, slide ordering, clarity, and executive messaging. List each recommendation with the slide number and the reason it strengthens the message. 

**Decision support presentation**

Create a presentation that supports a decision-making meeting. Include current state, challenges, options with pros and cons, a recommendation, and the specific decision points requiring leadership approval.  Add a final slide listing open questions and the owner for each. 

**Run through Word and then PowerPoint**

Using the procurement requirements in this document, draft an acquisition strategy for DGS leadership with objectives, approach, risks, and a timeline. Then create an eight-slide briefing deck from it.
